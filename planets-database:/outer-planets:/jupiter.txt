Saturn’s rings are huge—but super thin.
The rings stretch out hundreds of thousands of kilometers, yet in some places they’re only tens of meters thick.

Saturn is the least dense planet.
It’s so light (for its size) that it would float in water—if you could find a bathtub big enough.

Saturn has a crazy number of moons.
It has 140+ confirmed moons, including Titan, which is bigger than Mercury and has lakes of liquid methane.

There’s a hexagon-shaped storm at Saturn’s north pole.
It’s a massive, long-lasting six-sided storm—and scientists still aren’t completely sure why it keeps that shape.

Jupiter acts like a cosmic vacuum cleaner.
Its massive gravity pulls in or deflects asteroids and comets, helping protect the inner planets—especially Earth—from frequent impacts.
